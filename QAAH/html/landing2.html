Fractal Maze is a conjectural framework aimed at exploring the foundational nature of reality. It seeks to reconcile tensions between major physical theories while simultaneously addressing deep philosophical issues such as free will versus determinism, the nature of consciousness, epistemology, and other metaphysical domains.

Motivating challenges:

  • To uncover a deeper layer beneath quantum randomness

  • To transcend the conventional concept of space-time as N-dimensional differentiable manifold

  • To search for intrinsically non-computable physics

Core Hypotheses:

  1. The universe operates under an overarching optimization principle—there exists a fundamental quantity (analogous to action in physics) that must be minimized or optimized. This quantity may require redefinition beyond its classical formulation

  2. The fundamental structure of reality is discrete. Consequently, descriptions relying on real numbers (and thus on continuity) will eventually fail at a foundational level.

Importantly, proposing a discrete foundation does not imply simply discretizing space-time; rather, it suggests moving beyond space-time entirely. The proposed formulation is event-based: reality is constructed from discrete events and the relations between them. These events, represented as vertices in a graph, are not embedded in space-time. Instead, the graph encodes interactions among entities—initially analogous to particles in a Feynman diagram, but potentially representing more fundamental constituents.

The central principle governing these interactions is not causality in the conventional sense, but rather a structural optimization: the network is connected in such a way that it minimizes (or optimizes) the generalized action. Thus, the framework abandons space-time as a background and removes dimensionality and units from the fundamental description. Nodes do not represent regions in space or instants in time—they represent events, and the graph connecting them is dimensionless.

Contrast with Classical Mechanics:

In classical physics, the principle of least action in a continuous manifold implies that knowing the system at two points can fully determine its evolution—a consequence of the identity theorem. However, in a discrete and non-continuous structure, this determinism breaks down. Without continuity, the action no longer follows a smooth path; instead, it "branches" and becomes sensitive to the network's broader structure. Although there may still be a unique optimal path, its realization is influenced by a distributed context—not reducible to initial and final boundary conditions alone.

This framework bears resemblance to other approaches such as Quantum Loop Gravity (QLG), Wolfram Physics, and Quantum Graphity. However, key distinctions include:

  • The graph does not represent or approximate space-time. It has no intrinsic dimensionality or metric.

  • Connections between nodes are not based on causal ordering, but rather on optimizing the global action-like quantity.

  • Geometric properties of our world must emerge from the optimization, not be injected beforehand in the making.

In classical mechanics, when using the principle of least action in a smooth and continuous spacetime, the knowledge of two points or a reduced set of conditions completely fixes the trajectory for any instant (point on the curve). This is a consequence of the identity theorem. But when we pass to a structure where continuity is not defined or, you could say, is not continuous at any point. Here, since the theorem no longer holds, the action branches out, slips through the gaps and, although there is only one trajectory, all points, all over the environment, have something to say about it.

This idea admits being formally developed in several ways but bumps into calculations of exploding complexity. The networks -or graphs- it is built onto have a lot in common with those form other approaches: QLG, Wolfram Physics, Quantum Graphity… But there are some key differences:

  • The network is not or represent space-time, it has no dimensionality and it is dimensionless (no units in principle). The nodes are events, not chunks of space-time.

  • The relation between nodes is not causal: They are connected to optimize e quantity: our new action.

  • The properties of space-time must emerge from the optimization, not to be injected in the structure beforehand.

This leads to a conceptual shift where notions such as "when" and "where" lose meaning. The resulting picture aligns in spirit with ideas like superdeterminism, but potentially leaves room for an open-ended undecidable output.
